# Curriculum contract

## Audience

The primary learner is comfortable asking an AI chatbot for help but may not
know that an AI tool can work across files, tools, and several steps. The
curriculum assumes ordinary computer use, not coding, command-line experience,
or knowledge of AI system design.

The material must work for executives, household managers and caregivers,
legal, medical, and ministry professionals, students, educators, small teams,
nonprofits, researchers, creators, job seekers, field workers, and civic
volunteers. A role changes the scenario and safety boundary, not the core
learning goal.

## Purpose

Help people make day-to-day work more useful, repeatable, and reviewable with
desktop AI agents. Start with a real pain—repeated prompts, copying between
chat and files, scattered context, or inconsistent results—then show an
appropriate agent method.

## Progression

- **101: First useful result.** Choose an agent-sized task, create a safe
  workspace, give a clear brief, review permissions, check the output, and
  transfer the loop to personal work.
- **102: Reusable method.** Separate stable instructions from changing inputs,
  adopt or create a reusable workflow, test it across cases, and decide
  whether it is ready to share.
- **201: Dependable shared practice.** Recognize version, ownership,
  dependency, portability, update, and retirement needs; design a management
  model before selecting a product.

Learners may enter at the first level whose performance task they cannot yet
complete. Course attendance is not the prerequisite; observable evidence is.

## Scope and exclusions

The core is product-neutral. Platform pages link to official setup material
but do not own product instructions. The curriculum does not require or teach
a particular extension manager. It does not authorize sensitive information,
replace workplace policy, or transfer professional judgment to an agent.

## Design commitments

- Begin with a meaningful problem and an early visible result.
- Use fictional, public-safe practice materials.
- Teach safety inside each task, not only in a final warning.
- Demonstrate, practice with support, then require independent transfer.
- Make every outcome observable and every important output checkable.
- Offer choices in scenario and presentation while holding the learning goal
  steady.
- Use plain words, short steps, expected results, and recovery guidance.
- Keep one maintained source for concepts; derivative training links back to
  concept IDs and records the source version.
