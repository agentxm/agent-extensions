# How to facilitate the curriculum

## Before the session

1. Choose the promised outcomes and assessment evidence.
2. Confirm one supported product path and test the 101 lesson from a new
   account or clean workspace.
3. Provide the fictional practice files. Do not ask learners to bring private
   work to the first session.
4. Offer a no-install alternative or paired observation route for learners
   whose device, account, accessibility needs, or policy blocks setup.
5. Ask what learners already do with chat and where they repeat or relay work.
   Use those answers to connect new ideas to familiar practice.

## Two-hour 101 workshop

- **0-15 minutes:** Entry task. Learners sort chat, project, and agent examples
  and name one repeated-work pain.
- **15-30 minutes:** Demonstrate the full loop once. Narrate task boundaries,
  plan review, a rejected permission, and one source check.
- **30-75 minutes:** Learners complete the guided 101 lesson in pairs or alone.
  Give the smallest cue that restores progress.
- **75-95 minutes:** Debrief what the agent did, what the person still owned,
  and which check found the hidden disagreement.
- **95-115 minutes:** Independent transfer planning or a second fictional
  scenario. Each learner writes a new task brief and review plan.
- **115-120 minutes:** Exit evidence and next step.

## During practice

Use “show me what you expected” before correcting a learner. Normalize stopping
and rejecting permissions. Do not turn speed into the main sign of competence;
careful review may initially make a safe learner slower.

Offer instructions in text and spoken form, let learners work alone or with a
partner, and provide visible checkpoints. Keep the task goal constant while
allowing a choice of scenario or output presentation.

## Debrief questions

- Which part was easier than chat?
- Which part required more human judgment than expected?
- What did you verify outside the agent's answer?
- What permission or task boundary mattered?
- Would you repeat this method? What evidence supports that choice?

End with the learner's next safe task, not a catalog of advanced features.
