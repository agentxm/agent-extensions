# Research basis

The curriculum turns established design ideas into a practical learning path.
It does not claim that research on one population or subject proves an exact
effect for desktop-agent training.

## Alignment and observable performance

Learning outcomes, practice, and assessment use the same action. A learner who
must verify an agent's work practices verification and submits a check note;
they are not assessed only with a definition quiz. This follows constructive
alignment guidance and keeps completion tied to performance.

## Problem, demonstration, application, integration

Each level begins with a recognizable work problem, connects to prior chat
experience, demonstrates a complete method, provides supported application,
and ends with transfer to new work. The sequence reflects Merrill's problem-
centered principles and broader findings on active, goal-directed practice.

Retrieval, variation, self-explanation, and a later second run appear in 102 so
learners must recall and adapt the method instead of copying one example.

## Choice and access

Learners may choose a role-relevant scenario, read or hear instructions, work
alone or with support, and express a result in an appropriate format. The
learning goal and safety boundary stay constant. This applies Universal Design
for Learning as flexible access and action, not as lower expectations.

## Critical AI literacy and risk

The path teaches both effective use and critical evaluation. People inspect
sources, permissions, changes, limits, and unsupported claims. Risk controls
are contextual: the same drafting action requires different oversight when it
uses public event notes versus protected patient information.

## Reusable open source

Small concept documents, outcome IDs, templates, fictional fixtures, and
separate platform notes support reuse and adaptation. Derived courses can
select a route without copying and quietly changing the underlying truth.
