# How to pilot and improve the curriculum

Begin with the complete 101 path. Recruit 8 to 12 people across at least four
audience groups, including nontechnical learners, and use at least two agent
products or forms. Include people with varied accessibility needs where
possible; compensate them according to the host organization's practice.

## Observe

- Can the learner begin without unexplained technical help?
- Which words, steps, or permission messages cause hesitation?
- Can the learner predict what the agent will touch?
- Does the learner find the planted source disagreement?
- Can the learner write a new brief without copying the lesson?
- Does the method work across products without changing the outcome?
- Does the learner choose chat when chat is the better fit?

Record task completion, cues required, safety misses, check quality, time,
learner confidence, and later transfer. Separate product setup failures from
curriculum misunderstandings.

## Provisional release gates

Use these as starting targets, then revise from evidence:

- at least 80% complete the guided 101 task without facilitator rescue;
- at least 70% complete a new low-risk task brief and check independently;
- at least 80% correctly choose chat or an agent in varied task examples;
- no critical safety miss remains unexplained or uncorrected in the released
  path; and
- no core outcome depends on one product's name or interface.

Small samples reveal usability problems; they do not prove population-wide
effectiveness. Report the sample, setting, products, tasks, rubric, and
uncertainty with any rate.

## Improve

Fix blockers before adding more content. Prefer a clearer instruction, better
example, expected result, or recovery step over a longer explanation. Rerun the
affected task with new learners. Pilot 102 after 101 stabilizes, then pilot 201
with people who already share reusable workflows.
