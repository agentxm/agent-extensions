# Chat, projects, and desktop agents

These ways of using AI overlap, but they solve different problems.

| Way of working | Best for | What the person usually does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chat | A question, brainstorm, explanation, or one-off rewrite | Brings the information into the conversation and carries the answer back out |
| Saved project | Several conversations that share files, instructions, or a topic | Keeps related context together over time |
| Desktop agent | A result that requires files, tools, checks, or several connected steps | Gives the agent a bounded workspace, reviews its plan and actions, then checks the result |

A saved project may remember useful background, but storage and action are
different ideas. Keeping a set of conversations together does not by itself
mean the AI can operate on your working files or use your tools. Some products
combine all three ways of working in one app, so choose based on what the task
needs rather than the product name.

Use chat when the answer itself is the result. Use a desktop agent when the
result should live in your work: a changed document, organized folder, tested
piece of software, updated spreadsheet, or checked set of outputs.

A warning sign of a poor chat fit is **manual relay work**. You repeatedly
paste the same background, copy answers into several files, explain which
version is current, or ask the AI to remember rules that already live in your
workspace. That friction may be a good reason to try an agent.

Do not force every task into an agent. A two-minute question is often still a
two-minute chat. The goal is to remove avoidable handoffs, not to maximize
agent use.
