# Sensitive and high-stakes work

Desktop agents can reach more of the working environment than an ordinary
chat. That usefulness increases the need to know what information is present,
where it may go, and who is allowed to make the decision.

Sensitive information can include personal identifiers, health details,
student records, legal matters, financial accounts, employment records,
confessions or pastoral communications, unpublished business information, and
any material another person entrusted to you.

Before using such information, answer four questions from current policy:

1. Is this product and account approved for the information?
2. May the information be stored, uploaded, or processed where the agent runs?
3. Who may see the inputs, activity record, and outputs?
4. Which qualified person must review the work before anyone relies on it?

If any answer is unclear, use fictional or carefully de-identified material
for learning and stop the real task.

For law, medicine, finance, education, employment, and pastoral care, a useful
starting role is often **organize, research approved sources, compare,
format, or draft for review**. Do not ask an agent to make the final legal
strategy, diagnosis, treatment choice, eligibility decision, grade, hiring
decision, or spiritual judgment.

Removing a name may not be enough to protect a person. Dates, locations,
unusual events, and combinations of facts can identify someone. When in doubt,
create a fictional example that preserves the shape of the task without the
person's story.
