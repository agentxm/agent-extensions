# Risk and review levels

Use the highest level that applies to the information, action, or possible
harm.

| Level | Typical work | Minimum safeguards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Practice | Fictional or public information; new files in a practice folder | Review the brief and final output; preserve sources |
| 2. Real but reversible | Approved work information; drafts; changes that can be undone | Limit access; review the plan and all changes; keep a backup or history |
| 3. Outside effect | Sending, publishing, spending, account changes, or actions affecting another person | Require a person to approve the exact final action; confirm target and recovery |
| 4. Sensitive or high-stakes | Confidential, regulated, safety-critical, legal, medical, financial, employment, or pastoral matters | Follow governing policy; use approved systems only; require qualified human judgment; do not proceed when approval or safeguards are unclear |

Move up a level when several small risks combine. A harmless draft becomes a
Level 3 action when it is sent. Public facts become sensitive when combined in
a way that identifies a vulnerable person.

Higher risk does not always mean “never use AI.” It means the agent's role must
be narrower and the human review stronger. Research, organization, formatting,
and draft preparation may be appropriate where a final decision or diagnosis
is not.

If the downside is hard to reverse, difficult to notice, or borne by someone
who cannot review the work, slow down and seek the relevant policy or expert.
