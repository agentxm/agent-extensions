# Executive decision brief

**Situation:** A leader must choose whether to expand, delay, or narrow a
fictional service pilot. Updates are spread across meeting notes, a budget
summary, and customer-feedback themes.

**Agent fit:** The task requires comparing several files, keeping claims tied
to sources, and producing a reviewable brief. The leader still makes the
decision.

**Practice setup:** Create three fictional files with different dates. Include
one disagreement, one missing cost, and one action without an owner.

**Task brief:**

> Create `pilot-decision.md` for the leadership meeting. State the decision,
> compare expand/delay/narrow, list evidence for and against each option, show
> source filenames, and identify missing information. Keep it under two pages.
> Do not choose an option or change source files. Show the plan first.

**Checks:** Every factual claim names a source; disagreements remain visible;
unknowns are not turned into estimates; the brief separates facts from
recommendations; only one new file is created.

**Measure the gain:** Compare time spent locating evidence and copying it into
the brief. Note whether the result made missing information easier to see—not
only whether drafting was faster.

**Safety boundary:** Use approved business information only. Keep personnel,
customer, legal, and nonpublic financial details out of an unapproved product.
